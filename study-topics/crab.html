🦀 Crab

Comparing 5 varieties — safety, habitat, and how to eat each.

Not all crabs are equally safe to eat. This comparison covers the key contaminant risks, water quality of each variety's habitat, and how to eat them to minimize exposure.

Variety Comparison

Crab Habitat Water Quality Risk ☆ Key Concern How to Eat
Snow Crab 雪蟹 Deep cold water Class I (Excellent) ☆ Microplastics (MPs) — very low chemical pollution, but trace MPs accumulate in long-lived deep-sea species. Safest choice. Eat the leg meat only — virtually zero chemical load.
Stone Crab 石蟹 Near-shore rocky reefs Class II (Good) ☆☆ Trace mercury (Hg) — Florida near-shore waters are clean, but low-level heavy metal accumulation occurs at the bottom of the food chain. Very safe. Only the claw meat is eaten — this avoids the organs where metals concentrate.
Dungeness 珍宝蟹 Continental shelf sandy bottom Class II–III (Moderate) ☆☆☆ Domoic acid (Biotoxin) — seasonal warnings still issued on the West Coast as of early 2026. Natural toxin, not industrial pollution. Moderate. Avoid during red tide season — the meat itself is clean. Discard the innards.
Blue Crab 蓝蟹 Estuaries (e.g. Chesapeake Bay) Class III–IV (Poor) ☆☆☆☆ PCBs / Dioxins — heavily industrialized estuaries carry significant sediment contamination. Fat-soluble toxins concentrate strongly in the organs. High risk. Never eat whole-crab style (popular in Chinese cooking). White claw/body meat only.
Hairy Crab 大闸蟹 Industrial freshwater lakes Class III (Poor) ☆☆☆☆☆ Cadmium (Cd) / Pesticides / Industrial residues — bottom-feeding + organ-eating tradition puts it at the very top of the bioaccumulation chain. Highest risk. Safety depends entirely on strict government inspection. Eat in strict moderation.

General Rules

  • Avoid eating the tomalley (hepatopancreas / 蟹黄 / roe) in any high-risk variety — this is where contaminants concentrate most.
  • Stick to white muscle meat from the claws and body for the safest eating.
  • For Dungeness, check the California/Oregon/Washington shellfish safety hotline before buying during Oct–Mar.
  • Deep-sea species (Snow Crab) are the cleanest — their remote habitat means less industrial exposure.
