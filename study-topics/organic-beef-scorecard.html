Organic Beef Buying Guide

Source: Cornucopia Institute Organic Beef Scorecard · Scorecard last updated Oct 28, 2022

TL;DR

The Cornucopia Institute rates organic beef brands on a 1–5 cow scale based on pasture management, animal welfare, land stewardship, and transparency. Top brands (4–5 cows) are typically small family farms with real pasture access. Avoid 1-star brands like Verde Farms and Strauss Brands, which Cornucopia suspects of sourcing from factory-farm-scale operations. The one widely available 5-star brand is Panorama Organic at Whole Foods.

Rating Scale

RatingMeaning
⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 5 cows Gold standard. Small-to-medium family farms. Pasture and forage make up all or the majority of feed; not finished in confinement. Completed Cornucopia survey.
⭐⭐⭐⭐ 4 cows Ample pasture, definitive effort toward natural behaviors and land stewardship. Good choice.
⭐⭐⭐ 3 cows Didn't participate in survey but likely would rate 4–5 if they had. Confirmed to meet organic standards.
⭐⭐ 2 cows Less transparent. Difficult to confirm practices; possible concerns about compliance.
⭐ 1 cow Suspected factory-farm sourcing. Better than conventional, but lowest-rated organic brand.

Note: Cornucopia advises that even a 1-star organic brand beats conventional beef for avoiding the most toxic agrochemicals — but if you’re paying a premium, aim for 4–5 stars.

Online (ship to Bellevue) Stemple Creek Ranch ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Ships nationwide; order directly from stemplecreek.com

PCC vs Whole Foods: PCC has a higher sourcing floor — every piece of beef is certified organic or non-GMO; no conventional beef sold. Whole Foods allows conventional beef but has more rigorous animal welfare certification: all fresh beef must be independently certified to 100+ criteria (G.A.P./Certified Humane/AGW/ROC) with full farm traceability. Neither is strictly "better" — PCC raises the baseline, WF has more detailed third-party welfare auditing. — PCC Standards · Whole Foods Meat Standards
