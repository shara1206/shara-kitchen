Flour Brands & Quality: What to Buy

May 21, 2026 · Flour · Buying Guide

TL;DR

Unlike eggs and milk, there's no single independent watchdog rating flour brands. But there's strong consensus among bakers, food writers (Cook's Illustrated, Serious Eats, King Arthur's own bakers), and pro test kitchens: King Arthur wins on consistency and quality for everyday baking. Bob's Red Mill dominates whole grains and specialty flours. Central Milling, Janie's Mill, and Cairnspring Mills are the top tier for serious bread baking with regional/heritage grains. Generic store-brand flour is usable but inconsistent batch to batch.

The major brands

King Arthur Baking

Employee-owned · B-Corp certified · Vermont

Why it's the default recommendation: King Arthur tests every batch for protein content and guarantees consistency — their AP is always 11.7%, bread flour always 12.7%. That's unusual; most brands let protein drift by ~1%. Cook's Illustrated and most pro recipe developers test against King Arthur because it's the most reliable baseline.

Best products: Unbleached AP, Bread Flour, Whole Wheat, "00" Pizza Flour, Sir Galahad (their pro AP).

Downside: Slightly more expensive than supermarket brands. Hard to find some specialty lines outside their website.

Bob's Red Mill

Employee-owned · Stone-ground · Oregon

What it's known for: The widest lineup of specialty flours — whole grains, gluten-free, ancient grains (einkorn, spelt, kamut, teff). Stone-ground means more bran/germ retained, more flavor, shorter shelf life.

Best products: Stone-Ground Whole Wheat, Artisan Bread Flour, GF 1-to-1 Baking Flour, Spelt, Rye, Buckwheat.

Caveat: Their AP and bread flour get mixed reviews — some bakers find Bob's bread flour stickier and less consistent than King Arthur. For specialty flours, it's the best mainstream option.

Central Milling

Family-run since 1867 · Organic-focused · Utah

Why bakers love it: The flour that supplies many top US bakeries (Tartine, etc.). Excellent protein quality, not just protein quantity — the gluten in Central Milling flours is stretchier and stronger feeling. Their "Type 70" high-extraction flour is a favorite for sourdough.

Downside: Sold mostly in bulk (25 or 50 lb bags) and online. Not at supermarkets.

Cairnspring Mills

Skagit Valley, WA · Heritage Pacific NW grains · Stone-milled

What it's about: Regional, identity-preserved wheat (you can trace flour back to a specific farm). Fresh-milled, so it has more flavor than industrial flour. The Pacific Northwest's answer to Central Milling.

Gold Medal & Pillsbury

National · General Mills

The default supermarket brands. Reliable enough for everyday cookies and quick breads. Protein content varies more batch-to-batch than King Arthur. Gold Medal AP runs ~10.5%, which makes it slightly weaker than King Arthur AP for bread. Fine for most home baking, sub-par for serious bread.

White Lily

Southern US · Soft winter wheat

Beloved for biscuits and Southern baking. White Lily AP is essentially pastry flour (~9% protein), milled from soft winter wheat. Don't use it for yeast bread — it'll be dense. Do use it for biscuits, pancakes, and pie crust where tenderness matters.

Italian 00 brands (Caputo, Polselli, Le 5 Stagioni)

Imported · Pizza & pasta

For Neapolitan-style pizza, imported 00 makes a meaningful difference. Caputo Pizzeria (blue bag) is the gold standard — it's what most Italian pizzaiolos use. For pasta, Caputo Pasta Fresca or semolina-blend flours give the right bite.

Why no Cornucopia scorecard for flour? Flour quality is mostly about protein consistency, freshness, and milling style — not about animal welfare or outdoor access. The questions a scorecard would answer (is it organic? is the wheat heritage? is it regional?) are visible on the bag.
