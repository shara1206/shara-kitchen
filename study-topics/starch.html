What decides how a starch behaves

Four properties explain almost every substitution question.

Amylose ratio

Higher → firmer gel that sets and later weeps (mung bean, corn). Lower / more amylopectin → chewy, stretchy, stays soft (tapioca, glutinous rice).

Gelatinization temp

Where granules swell and the liquid thickens. Root and tuber starches thicken earlier; cereal starches need a full boil.

Clarity

Root, tuber and legume starches cook clear; cereal starches (corn, wheat, rice) stay cloudy.

Behavior on cooling

Sets into a gel, stays a fluid sauce, or turns springy — this decides custard vs glaze vs noodle.

By kitchen job

🥣

Thickening

Clear, quick, at the end → potato starch. Holds up to long simmering and reheating → cornstarch.

🍮

Setting a gel

Cornstarch for custard and pie filling; mung bean and water chestnut starch for jellies and steamed cakes. More amylose = firmer set.

🍗

Fry coating

Potato starch = lightest and crispiest; sweet potato starch = thick, craggy; cornstarch in between. Often cut 1:1 with flour.

🥩

Velveting meat

Locks in moisture and forms a protective film so meat stays tender. Potato or cornstarch at roughly 2–5% of meat weight.

🎂

Softening baked goods

Remove 2 Tbsp from 1 cup all-purpose flour and replace with cornstarch ≈ cake flour — softer cakes and cookies.

🧋

Chewy texture

Comes from high-amylopectin starches: tapioca and sweet potato — boba, cheese bread, glass noodles.

🥟

Translucent wrappers

Wheat starch scalded with boiling water, plus some tapioca for elasticity — har gow and crystal dumplings.

🍬

Moisture absorbing / anti-caking

US powdered sugar typically contains ~3% cornstarch so it doesn't clump.

Why it failed

  • Custard won't thicken, or turns watery overnightIt wasn't boiled. Egg yolk contains amylase that digests starch until it's inactivated near 100°C — bring it to a full bubble and hold ~1 min.
  • Sauce thinned after thickeningPotato starch breaks down with prolonged heat or vigorous stirring. Add it last, stir gently, serve.
  • Weeping after freezingCornstarch retrogrades hard. For anything going in the freezer use tapioca, arrowroot or a modified starch.
  • Acidic filling won't setAcid weakens starch thickening. For lemon-style fillings, thicken first, then stir in the juice.
  • LumpsAlways slurry in cold water first (about 1 : 2 starch to water) and stir it in while the liquid simmers.
