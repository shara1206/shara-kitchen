Organic Egg Brand Comparison

Source: The Cornucopia Institute Organic Egg Scorecard · May 21, 2026

TL;DR

The Cornucopia Institute is the most trusted independent rater of organic eggs in the US. It's a non-profit, accepts no industry funding, and rates 150+ organic egg brands on 28 criteria (flock size, real outdoor access, transparency, etc.) on a 1–5 egg scale. Its takeaway: many big-name "organic" brands rate only 1–2 eggs because they're effectively industrial operations with concrete porches passing as "outdoor access."

Why Cornucopia is the source to trust

Cornucopia is a 501(c)(3) non-profit that has been auditing organic egg producers since 2010. They send detailed questionnaires to every certified organic egg brand, do on-site investigations, and publicly publish the methodology. The New York Times, NPR, and most food-journalism sources cite their scorecard as the standard.

The rating scale:

RatingMeaning
5 eggsExemplary — beyond organic. Small/medium farms, mobile housing, ample real pasture.
4 eggsExcellent — true outdoor access and high welfare.
3 eggsGood — meets organic standards, family farms with outdoor runs.
2 eggsFair — some compliance questions.
1 egg"Industrial organic" — no meaningful outdoor access, low transparency.

How to use this

  • If price doesn't matter: Vital Farms or Alexandre — both 5-egg and broadly available.
  • For everyday value: Pete & Gerry's or Organic Valley — solid middle tier, in nearly every store.
  • Skip: Generic "organic" store brands — they look reassuring but rate near the bottom.
  • Check the full scorecard: cornucopia.org/scorecard/eggs — searchable by brand, updated regularly.
