Why You Can’t Get All Three

Organic + Pasture-Raised + Air-Chilled on one package essentially does not exist at retail, and it is structural rather than bad luck. Air-chilling needs a large processing line that only mid- to large-scale plants can justify; genuinely pasture-raised birds come from small farms that use small or mobile slaughter facilities, which water-chill or freeze. Organic certification follows the large processors.

Small scale

Real pasture, high welfare scores, water-chilled or frozen, sold at farmers markets or shipped direct.

— mutually exclusive —

Large scale

Air-chilled, organic certified, but “free-range” at best, in every supermarket.

The scorecard confirms it: of the nine 5-star brands on Cornucopia’s organic poultry scorecard, not one has national distribution, and the only West Coast entry is Rockside Ranch (Northern California only). The 5-star tier is structurally a tier of local small farms.

Breed: The Column No Label Covers

Organic certification says nothing about breed. Neither does pasture-raised, free-range, air-chilled, or No Antibiotics Ever. A bird can carry every certification on the shelf and still be the same fast-growing hybrid as the cheapest chicken in the case.

“Breed” is also the wrong word. Commercial broilers are proprietary multi-line hybrids, and the genetics behind essentially every chicken sold in the US come from just two companies: Aviagen (owned by Germany’s EW Group; sells the Ross, Arbor Acres and Hubbard lines) and Cobb-Vantress (owned by Tyson; sells the Cobb lines). Brand and certification vary enormously; the underlying genetics barely do.

Read the last two rows together. Between 2000 and 2025 the age at slaughter barely moved — 47 to 47.5 days — while market weight rose 32% (5.03 → 6.63 lb) and feed conversion fell from 1.95 to 1.70. A quarter century of breeding went entirely into putting more meat on the bird in the same number of days. That is the bird behind almost every label in the table above.

The one certification that does regulate breed is the slow-growth requirement shared by the Better Chicken Commitment and Global Animal Partnership. After a University of Guelph study of more than 7,500 birds across 16 genetic strains, GAP published the strains eligible for welfare certification:

Aviagen

Ranger Classic · Ranger Gold · Ranger Premium

Hubbard

JA757 · JA787 · JACY87 · REDBRO · Redbro M

Cobb-Vantress

Cobb-Sasso 200

Selected for slower growth, higher bone density, better mobility, and low rates of muscle myopathy — the defects that come with pushing weight gain.

So if breed is what you are after, the thing to look for is not the organic seal. It is either a Better Chicken Commitment / GAP slow-growth claim, or a producer running its own slower line — Bell & Evans converted 100% of its production to its Das Klassenbester line by the end of 2018.

Reading the Shelf at Whole Foods

GAP step Counts as pasture-raised?
Step 1–3 ✘ No — indoor systems with varying enrichment
Step 4 ✔ Yes — pasture-based systems start here; outdoor and pasture access both required, third-party audited
Step 5 / 5+ ✔ Yes — stricter again

The rule: USDA Organic + GAP Step 4 or above. This beats reading the words “Pasture-Raised” off the package, because that phrase has no federal definition and no audit behind it — the GAP step does.
